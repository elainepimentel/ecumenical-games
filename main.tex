\section{Introduction}
\begin{itemize}
\item Describe Hintika games for classical and intuitionistic logics
\item How can we combine tactics so that the classical and intuitionistic do not collapse?
\end{itemize}

Outline:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Classical cost of proofs:
\begin{itemize}
\item Start with the sequent system with stoup. 
\item Define a game where the {\em state} is only one sequent. With rules with only one premise, substitute one for the other. With two, the player ``You'' chooses the premise.
\item Prove adequacy.
\item Define and add classical budget to the games. Each time a classical rule is used, ``pay'' 1. The budget should be a string, and ``paying'' means appending 1 or 0 to the string, depending if the move is classical or intuitionistic. 
\item Define a labelled sequent system, with costs as labels. Difficult question: how to combine them?
\item Try to prove cut-elimination, etc.
\end{itemize}
\item Satisfiability and validity games.
\begin{itemize}
\item Start with an ecumenical game on satisfiability where the players ``I'' and ``You'' can take the roles of $\Op, \Ppi,\Ppc$ for opponent, proponent on a classical role, proponent on an intuitionistic role respectively. 
\item Define the satisfiability game on Kripke structures. This will generate a labeled game.
\item Move to a (labeled) validity game.
\item Extract a (labeled) sequent system. 
\item Prove that this system is equivalent to the one with stoup.
\end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}


\section{Pure semantics proposal}
 In 2021, Marin et al. \cite{pureViewEcumenicalModalities}\footnote{see \cite{DBLP:conf/dali/MarinPPS20, marin2022separabilityharmonyecumenicalsystems, pereira2022ecumenicalnaturaldeductionstoup, TourEcumenicalpimentel_et_al:LIPIcs.CALCO.2023.3} for further discussion} proposed a pure sequent calculus system equivalent to Prawitz's ecumenical system. Their motivation departs from the observation that ``Although being a powerful tool for describing proof-theoretical properties of Prawitz’ ecumenical logic, LE is not satisfactory as a logical system since it is not pure" \cite{TourEcumenicalpimentel_et_al:LIPIcs.CALCO.2023.3}. Their strategy in building a pure system is to (i) work with ``a new notion of polarities for ecumenical formulas" \cite{pureViewEcumenicalModalities}\footnote{
%\begin{definition}[Positive and Negative formulas]
A formula is said to be \textbf{negative} if its main connective is classical and \textbf{positive} otherwise.
%\end{definition}
}; 
and (ii) use Girard's notion of stoup \cite{Girard_1991} to control rule applications by separating contexts into bins. In their system, sequents have the form $\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta;\Sigma$ where $\Gamma, \Delta, \Sigma$ represent multisets of formulae, and the \textit{stoup} $\Sigma$, is limited to containing at most one formula. Two structural rules, \textit{Store} and \textit{Dereliction}, control how formulas move in and out of the \textit{stoup} area:

    \begin{center}
    \begin{minipage}[b]{.3 \textwidth}
        \begin{prooftree}
          \AxiomC{$\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta,P; P$}
          \UnaryInfC{$\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta,P;.$}   
        \end{prooftree}
    \end{minipage}
    \begin{minipage}[b]{.3 \textwidth}
        \begin{prooftree}
        \AxiomC{$\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta, N;.$}
        \UnaryInfC{$\Gamma \Rightarrow \Delta; N$}
        \end{prooftree}
    \end{minipage}
    \end{center}

Our proposal is to develop a game semantics with respect to the pure ecumenical system described above with two players, me and you, and three roles: an opponent \textbf{$O$}, a classical proponent \textbf{$P^c$} and an intuitionistic proponent \textbf{$P^i$}. The usual restrictions for intuitionistic dialogues are imposed only to $P^i$.
